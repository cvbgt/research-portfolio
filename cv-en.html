Research Profile

Intelligent Actuators and Sensors from Diverse Physical Principles

I implement actuation and sensing principles as devices and components, then develop them into self-contained modules that improve system integration and maturity. I validate the modules in wearable systems and robots.

  • An actuator or sensor becomes a usable component only when its physical principle, structure, circuitry, and control work together.
  • I implement diverse actuation and sensing mechanisms as devices that can be fabricated and tested.
  • I evaluate performance, lifetime, repeatability, and power, signal, and mechanical interfaces together.
  • I develop validated devices into self-contained modules and evaluate them in wearable systems and robots under physical contact.

Doctoral Thesis

Novel Signal Modulation Methods to Improve the Function of Electrostatic Actuators and Sensors in Wearable Systems

Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering, Ulsan National Institute of Science and Technology (UNIST) · 2024-02-15
  • Wearable actuators must be light and compliant, while sensors must be distributed over large areas.
  • Motor–gearbox actuators are heavy and rigid, and sensor arrays become harder to scale as every additional channel increases wiring.
  • This dissertation implements muscle- and skin-like functions at the device level and unifies actuation and sensing through electrical signal modulation.
  • Muscle functions are implemented through phase modulation, and skin-like sensing through frequency modulation.

Core Contribution

  • A shared electrical-signal-domain methodology addresses actuation and sensing rather than relying on materials alone.
  • Electrostatic actuation reproduces isometric support and deformation generation without electromagnetic force.
  • Frequency-domain sensing enables tactile arrays whose wiring does not increase with the number of channels.
